# True Sidereal POC: MTZ Verification Worksheet

Generated by `scripts/generate-worksheet.ts`

## How to use this worksheet

1. For each chart below, enter the birth data into the MTZ calculator
   at https://masteringthezodiac.com/calculator (or True Sky / Stellarium).
2. Record the MTZ result in the **MTZ Observed** column.
3. Mark the **Agreement** column:
   - `exact`: sign and degree match within 1°
   - `sign-match`: same sign, degree differs or not available from MTZ
   - `sign-disagree`: different sign assignment
   - `transition-zone`: disagree, but computed value is within 3° of boundary
4. Note any discrepancies in the **Notes** column.
5. After filling in all charts, transfer verified values to `data/verified-charts.ts`
   and set `verified: true`.

## Important: what MTZ shows vs. what we compute

- MTZ typically shows **sign name** and **degree within sign** (e.g., "Gemini 2°").
- MTZ does **not** show raw J2000 ecliptic longitude.
- If MTZ shows only a sign name with no degree, record the sign and leave degree blank.
- Do **not** assume numerical longitude agreement when only signs match.
- MTZ uses topocentric Moon. Our POC also applies topocentric correction.
- MTZ ascendant conventions may differ (see Discrepancy Analysis below).

## Appendix: Known Sources of Potential Discrepancy

### 1. Coordinate Frame

Our POC computes positions in J2000 mean ecliptic (ECL) via:
`GeoVector(EQJ) -> Rotation_EQJ_ECL -> RotateVector -> atan2`.

If MTZ uses a different ecliptic reference (ecliptic of date, or a different
epoch), longitudes will differ by ~0.01°/year from J2000. For a 2024 chart,
the ECT-vs-ECL offset is ~0.34°. This should not change sign assignments
except within ~0.5° of a boundary.

### 2. Topocentric Moon

Our POC applies topocentric correction for the Moon (up to ~1° parallax).
MTZ also recommends topocentric. If there is a Moon sign disagreement,
check whether the computed longitude is within 1° of the boundary -- if so,
the topocentric correction implementation may differ slightly.

### 3. Ascendant Conventions

Our ascendant is computed from the standard formula:
`ASC = atan2(-cos(ARMC), sin(ARMC)*cos(ε) + tan(φ)*sin(ε)) + π`
then rotated from ECT to J2000 ECL.

Possible convention differences with MTZ:
- MTZ may use a slightly different obliquity source or nutation model.
- The GAST (Greenwich Apparent Sidereal Time) precision varies across libraries.
- A 1-second time error shifts the ascendant by ~0.004°, so timezone/UT1
  discrepancies can accumulate to ~0.25° in practice.

### 4. Boundary Table Version

Our POC uses the Midpoint Method boundary table version 2025.07 (post-July 2025
revision). If MTZ has updated boundaries since, or if the version you are
comparing against predates the revision, boundaries for Aries, Taurus, Gemini,
and Cancer may differ by up to ~3°.

### 5. Rounding

Our POC rounds `degreeInSign` to 2 decimal places and `eclipticLongitude`
to 4 decimal places. MTZ typically displays integer degrees. A computed
value of 29.8° in one sign might display as 0° in the next sign on MTZ
if they round differently.

### 6. Time Input

Birth times in this worksheet are given in UTC. When entering into MTZ,
use the **local time** and **timezone** shown in the birth data table.
A timezone error of 1 hour shifts the Sun by ~0.04° and the Moon by ~0.5°.
The ascendant shifts by ~15° per hour -- timezone errors are the most common
source of large ascendant discrepancies.
